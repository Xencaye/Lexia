## 2. LibriVox terms

- https://librivox.org/pages/public-domain/ (read 2026-11-17): "all our recordings are public domain (definitely in the USA, and maybe in your country as well ...)"; "anyone can use all our recordings however they wish (even to sell them)"; "if you record for LibriVox, all your recordings will be donated to the public domain"; "you may do whatever you like with our recordings - you don't need permission"; "there is no need to credit LibriVox, although ... we much prefer if you do credit us (with a link to our site)". Limits: PD status is by US law; "not necessarily in other countries"; outside the USA check the work's status.
- https://librivox.org/pages/volunteer-for-librivox/: "All our recordings (including yours, if you volunteer for us) are also donated into the public domain."
- LibriVox wiki https://wiki.librivox.org/index.php/Copyright_and_Public_Domain (revision 33429, read 2026-11-17): readers agree "to release the audio files you make into the public domain"; users "do not need to give credit to the individual reader/writer/creator or to LibriVox"; its list of known re-uses includes "Used LibriVox recordings to train AI models" (I confirmed in the page history that this line was added in the last edit, 5 Aug 2026). The page ends with "You really have to let go!" Exceptions on the page: PD basis is US law; translations, edited editions, new introductions can have their own copyright and are not recorded unless PD; LibriVox "takes a very cautious approach" but cannot guarantee non-US status.
- So: LibriVox policy explicitly accepts AI-model training and any commercial use. It is NOT a guarantee for each recording (a reader could have broken the rules, a text could be wrongly cleared) and it says nothing on voice or personality rights. It is the project's policy, not a signed contract by each reader (the volunteer page above is the only "terms" I found; I did not find a signed per-reader agreement text).
- Cori Samuel / LibriVox user "Cori" (https://wiki.librivox.org/index.php/User:Cori): joined LibriVox Nov 2005, "I've made several commercial recordings, available at audible.com and audible.co.uk". She is a working narrator (OverDrive also lists her). This raises the reputational and "livelihood" sensitivity of a recognisable clone of her voice (see risk 2). The wiki page does not mention TTS or AI. I found NO public statement by her about TTS voices from her recordings, and no takedown or complaint about Piper's cori, hifi_tts_92 or similar (searches of the web, LibriVox forum, GitHub; absence of evidence only). A LibriVox forum thread (https://forum.librivox.org/viewtopic.php?p=2304541 area, via search summary) shows some readers worry about consent and livelihood in general; r/librivox Feb 2024 thread shows mixed opinions. Secondary.
- Precedent: LJ Speech (https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/): "Recordings by Linda Johnson from LibriVox", "All text, audio, and annotations are in the public domain". NVIDIA Hi-Fi TTS (https://www.openslr.org/109/, arXiv 2104.01497): "based on public audiobooks from LibriVox", CC BY 4.0, 10 speakers. LibriTTS/LibriSpeech are also LibriVox-derived (CC BY 4.0). Piper's own voices ljspeech, hifi_tts_92, libritts, and the new cori medium are built from these.

## 3. Voice / personality rights (all secondary sources unless noted)

- US federal: NO FAKES Act of 2026 (S. 4591, H.R. 8915) NOT law. Senate Judiciary approved it by voice vote 18 Jun 2026; on 30 Sep 2026 Sen. Cruz blocked unanimous consent (Forbes, 2026-09-30); latest source I found is mid-Oct 2026; 119th Congress ends 3 Jan 2027. It targets unauthorised AI "digital replicas" of voice/likeness, with notice-and-takedown for platforms and liability for producing/distributing; exceptions for First Amendment uses (news, parody, criticism) and nonprofit research (Holland & Knight, https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name). Status after Oct 2026 unverified; check congress.gov.
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (in force 1 Jul 2024): extends right of publicity to "voice" incl. simulated voice; liability for distributing an "algorithm, software, tool, or other technology" whose "primary purpose or function" is producing an identifiable individual's voice, with a knowledge element; exemptions in Tenn. Code 47-25-1107 (WSGR, Latham summaries). I could NOT fetch the statute text (Justia, tn.gov blocked: 403). The "primary purpose" phrase is undefined. A general TTS app is not primarily built to produce one named person's voice, but a voice labelled "Cori" is a one-person voice model; applicability unverified.
- California AB 1836 (deceased performers' digital replicas) and AB 2602 (contract terms for replicas in lieu of work), in force 1 Jan 2025 (Perkins Coie, Manatt, Skadden summaries): about deceased performers and employment/performance contracts. Cori and the other readers are living volunteers and no contract exists; not on point.
- New York: Lehrman v. Lovo, S.D.N.Y. No. 1:24-cv-03770, ruling 10 Jul 2025 (secondary): copyright and Lanham Act claims over a voice clone mostly dismissed (voice is not copyrightable, training claim unexplained); claims under N.Y. Civil Rights Law 50-51 (use of a person's voice for trade/advertising without consent) and contract survived. Closest precedent: a recognisable voice clone used commercially can raise a state publicity claim even when copyright fails. There the voices were sold as a commercial voice product.
- Older US: Midler v. Ford, Waits v. Frito-Lay (voice imitation in advertising) - known from memory, not re-checked here.
- UK: no general personality/image right; passing off (Irvine v Talksport, Fenty v Arcadia) needs goodwill, misrepresentation, damage; voice cases untested (Sim v Heinz failed); a government consultation on digital replicas reported for summer 2026 (unconfirmed) (Reed Smith, Pinsent Masons, CMS summaries). Cori Samuel is UK-based (moved to Australia per her wiki page): a UK/AU claim looks weak for a non-commercial or generic assistive use.
- EU/GDPR: a voice recording is personal data (identifiable speaker); "biometric data" special-category status only applies when processed for unique identification. EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf, adopted 17 Dec 2024): whether an AI model trained on personal data is anonymous is judged case by case. A single-speaker TTS voice reproduces that person's voice by design, so it is probably NOT anonymous. Lawfulness of the original training lies with Bryce; a distributor is exposed mainly if a data-protection authority or the reader complains. Legal basis would be arguable (the recordings were deliberately made public by the reader, with a PD dedication). No case found against any TTS voice on this ground.
- Likelihood summary: these laws target (a) impersonation / deepfakes / unauthorised "replicas", (b) commercial exploitation (ads, products selling the person's voice), (c) deceased performers (CA), (d) performance contracts. A free assistive app that reads text in a generic voice, credited by dataset and reader name, is a long way from impersonation; the realistic risk is a complaint by a reader (Cori is a commercial narrator) not a successful claim. "Commercial use by others who redistribute GPL code" widens the exposure for those downstream users, in particular ELVIS/NY-style claims if they market the voice by her name.

## 4. GPL-3.0 and public domain files

- GPLv3 section 5 (https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt, read 2026-11-17): a compilation of a covered work with other separate and independent works on a distribution medium is an "aggregate" if the compilation does not limit users' rights "beyond what the individual works permit"; inclusion "does not cause this License to apply to the other parts of the aggregate." The model files are data read at run time, not linked code: an aggregate. Public domain adds no condition, so there is no conflict. Lexia already ships PD voices (ljspeech, norman) the same way.
- Caveat: "public domain" stated only on a web page, without CC0 text. In civil-law countries (e.g. Germany) a copyright holder cannot always waive rights; a PD statement is normally read as a broad permission. Piper's own repo licence is MIT (HF README `license: mit`), which does not matter for per-voice data. Does Bryce own any copyright in the .onnx? Probably little (generated weights); anyway he has dedicated it.
- Notice/credit is not legally required by any source found, but LibriVox "much prefer[s]" credit and it is good practice (see section 6).

## 6. Conclusions

(a) Cori medium (old, 5041a7a) as Lexia's default Voice: Copyright/licence: yes, I found no obstacle. Bryce says public domain; LibriVox policy says every recording is a PD donation and lists AI training as accepted; SHA-256 matches. Pin to the exact 5041a7a file and sha. The remaining risks are not copyright: (i) Cori Samuel is a working commercial narrator whose voice would become Lexia's default (most prominent, most identifiable use), (ii) no statement from her either way, (iii) her UK/AU residence makes a US publicity claim unlikely. Because it is the default Voice, a courtesy contact with her before release (she blogs at piratelibrary.com, is on the LibriVox forum) would cut the main risk cheaply. Quality note: if the default must also be low-risk, ljspeech (the PD benchmark) or norman is the safer default; they are already shipped.

(b) Others:
- Kristin, John, Norman, Cori high: same position as Cori medium; ship/list OK on copyright. Reader names for John and Norman unknown, so credit them as "LibriVox volunteer readers" (by the model name) and do not claim names you cannot verify. John is a fine-tune of Kristin (so also her voice characteristics at least in part), say so in the notice.
- ManyVoice (mv2): ship only with care. 16 identifiable real readers, names are only in sample file names, and one file bundles 16 identities (cannot remove one on request); the page says "I used various tools to improve the sound quality". Listing it for download is possible; shipping it as part of the installer is not worth it. Lexia's Voice model also needs per-speaker handling for multi-speaker models (code question, not checked).
- LJSpeech: PD, reader Linda Johnson named on keithito.com; ship OK (already shipped).
- Excluded, one line each: Bryce = author's own voice, base unreleased (permission is his own); Clean100 = LibriTTS-R, CC BY 4.0 attribution to dataset authors required; Jenny (Dioco) = attribution, CC BY-like terms on the Kaggle page.

(c) Suggested notice (adapt, keep British English): "Voice <name>: trained by Bryce Beattie (https://brycebeattie.com/files/tts/) on public-domain recordings by LibriVox (https://librivox.org) volunteer reader(s) <reader name if known>. LibriVox recordings are dedicated to the public domain; Bryce Beattie releases the voice model as public domain. Voices are listed by the name of the model. Lexia is not endorsed by, and has no connection with, the reader." For Cori: "Voice of Cori Samuel, a LibriVox volunteer reader (not endorsed by her)". For John: "fine-tuned from Kristin". Put it in licenses/components/voice-<name>.txt (already required by notices.cmake), the Voices page "about this voice" text and the download sheet. State the SHA-256 and source URL there too. Do not call any voice by a living person's name in marketing/feature text; do not allow cloning features.

(d) Risks, ranked:
1. Provenance depends on Bryce's own word and LibriVox's general policy: no per-reader consent record, no reader/book list, a single web page with "public domain" and no licence text; a single recording that was not truly PD (LibriVox itself says it cannot guarantee non-US status) cannot be excluded. Mitigation: keep pinned hashes and source URLs, store the page and LibriVox terms in the repo (docs/notes), ask Bryce by email for a one-line confirmation (CC0) and the reader list.
2. Reader sensitivity / voice rights, especially Cori Samuel (commercial narrator, now default voice): possible complaint or takedown request; legal exposure low for a free assistive app credited this way, but ELVIS Act (primary-purpose clause, undefined), N.Y. Civil Rights Law 50-51 (Lehrman v. Lovo) and a future NO FAKES Act (not law as of mid-Oct 2026) are the live theories, mostly aimed at commercial or deceptive use. Higher for downstream commercial redistributors than for Lexia. Mitigation: contact her, have a documented removal procedure (voice removable at a user's/owner's request, adoption logic already keeps user copies), no marketing by her name.
3. GDPR (EU users/distribution): single-speaker model is probably personal data (EDPB Opinion 28/2024, case-by-case); complaint risk low, no enforcement against a TTS voice found.
4. Bryce's closing note "legal and ethical purpose" could be read as a use restriction; GPL section 10 forbids further restrictions on GPL code, but the voice is a separate aggregate item, and the page's licence line says only "public domain". Low; clarify with Bryce.
5. Upstream drift: HF main replaced cori medium on 2026-11-09 with a CC BY 4.0 model (attribution to NVIDIA/dataset needed if anyone updates the pin); do not follow "latest". Also Piper's maintainer may remove old voices; keep your own copy and hash.
6. Integrity: no signed hashes from Bryce; the SHA-256 match with HF is a cross-check of two sources, not authentication.
